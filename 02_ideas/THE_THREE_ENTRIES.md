# ⚠️ READ THIS FIRST — the rule risk that could void all three

The 2026 T&Cs describe **teams of up to 4 members, or individuals**. The registration form asks for Team-or-Individual, a team name, and **one track**.

**Three people registering individually under the same team name is ambiguous.** It could be read as:
- three independent individuals who happen to share a name → probably fine, or
- one team submitting three entries → possibly not permitted

**Action, this week, in writing:** ask the organisers directly — *"May three members of the same team each submit one entry in a different track under a shared team name, or is one submission per team the rule?"* Keep the reply.

**If it is allowed**, there is a second, deliberate choice: do the three entries publicly reference each other, or stand alone?

> **Recommendation: link them.** Three unrelated apps reads as spray-and-pray. Three products built from one argument reads as a company. The thesis below is strong enough to make each entry *bigger* for being part of it — and "we submitted three because South Africa's problem is bigger than one app" is a good line, not a defensive one.

**If it is not allowed**, the fallback is obvious: one entry (Umoya), and the other two concepts become the "where this goes next" slide.

# PART 1 — WHICH TRACK IS UMOYA?

## ✅ Track 1 — Everyday Essentials

Not a close call once you look at what Umoya actually *does* on screen.

**The case for it:**

| Track 1's own scope | What Umoya does |
|---|---|
| Prepaid electricity & water | Forecasts consumption, times purchases, warns before the advance trap |
| Airtime & data | Kills the 3.5× data poverty premium; group-buys bundles |

| Household & family money | *"Send gogo R200 on the 1st"* — the most common money instruction in the country |
| Everyday transacting | Voice, USSD, offline — the actual daily surface |

Every demo scene in Umoya is a Track 1 scene. The forecasting layer is *literally* about electricity and data. The mandate layer is *literally* about bills and family transfers.

**The case against the other two:**
- **Track 2 (Entertainment & Lifestyle)** — no. Umoya is not fun, and pretending it is would weaken it.

**The bonus you get by choosing correctly:** Track 1 is the most crowded track and will be full of bill-payment apps and budgeting tools. **Contrast is your friend.** Umoya next to a bill-splitter is not a fair fight, and the judges will feel the gap immediately. Being the biggest thing in a crowded room beats being the only thing in an empty one — *provided you actually are.*

**One caveat that could change this:** if the organisers confirm judging is **per-track** rather than overall, and Track 1 is visibly oversubscribed on the day, the calculus shifts. **That question is still unanswered and is still the highest-value thing to ask.** But you cannot credibly move Umoya to Entertainment, so this changes little in practice — it matters far more for the other two entries.

# PART 2 — THE NAME

## Your instinct about inclusivity is right. The evidence resolves it in Umoya's favour.

isiZulu is the largest home language at **24.4%** — the largest, but nowhere near a majority. Naming a national product in one language group's word is a real risk, and you were right to raise it.

**But "umoya" is not a Zulu word. It's a Bantu root, and it survives across both major South African language families.**

| Family | Languages | Form | Home-language share |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nguni** | isiZulu, isiXhosa, siSwati, isiNdebele | *umoya* — breath, air, wind, spirit | 24.4 + 16.3 + 2.8 + 1.7 = **45.2%** |
| **Sotho-Tswana** | Sesotho, Setswana, Sepedi | *moya* / *mowa* — spirit, wind, breath | 7.8 + 8.3 + 10.0 = **26.1%** |
| | | **Combined** | **≈71%** |

**Roughly seven in ten South Africans hear their own word.** That is not a compromise — it is a genuinely rare property, and it is a *pitch line*:

> *"Umoya is one of the very few words that means the same thing in seven of South Africa's twelve official languages. That's the whole point of the product."*

⚠️ **Verify this on the team before it goes on a slide.** I have confirmed *umoya = spirit* in isiXhosa from a dictionary source and the Nguni forms are secure; the Sotho-Tswana forms I am confident about but have not confirmed from a primary lexicographic source. **Get a Sesotho and a Setswana speaker to sign it off.** If either is wrong, the line dies but the name survives — 45% is still the largest single bloc.

## Why NOT "Noma"

**"Noma" is isiZulu-specific.** isiXhosa uses *okanye* (or) and *nokuba* (even if). So Noma inherits exactly the exclusion problem you were worried about, while Umoya solves it. The "even if" meaning is lovely — **keep it as the tagline, not the name.**

> ### **UMOYA** — *even if.*

That gives you the inclusive name *and* the resilience promise, and the tagline works in English for the judges who don't speak either.

**Other properties worth having:** pronounceable by everyone (oo-MOY-ah), no negative homonym I could find, not an existing SA fintech brand, and it means *breath / air / spirit* — which is literally what the product is: **the intelligence that lives in the air, not in your hand.**

**Team name:** `South Africa-Umoya` is clean and ties all three entries together. Alternative if you want the team name distinct from the product: `South Africa-Moya`.

# PART 3 — TRACK 2 ENTRY

## 🚀 AMAZWI — *the voices*
### The game where being South African pays

> **Alternative names:** **VUKA** ("wake up" — punchier, more youth-native, Nguni-shared) · **MZANSI VOICE** (universally used across every language group, zero exclusion risk, weaker as a brand).

### The one sentence

**A game where young South Africans are paid, in MoMo, cents at a time, for the one asset they own that the world's AI companies desperately need and cannot buy: their language.**

### The facts that make it explosive

- **4.7 million unemployed South Africans aged 15–34.** Youth unemployment **45.8%**, up 2.0pp in one quarter. **50.5% of employed 15–24-year-olds work informally.**
- **Google's WAXAL push (Feb 2026) released 11,000+ hours across 27 African languages — and not one of them is a South African official language.** Google is funding East and West Africa. South Africa was skipped.
- **Swivuriso**, the only substantial SA corpus, is 3,016 hours across 7 languages — and **excludes Afrikaans, Sepedi and siSwati entirely.**
- **Code-switched South African speech data barely exists.** The June 2026 frontier code-switching benchmark tested seven leading ASR systems on **zero African languages.** Code-switching costs monolingual ASR **30–50% WER** — and "ngicela i-data" is how the entire country actually speaks.
- **Google Cloud gets isiXhosa wrong 56.71% of the time.** That is not a model problem. It is a data problem.
- **MTN has publicly bet on AI** — Huawei MoU, a Technology Innovation Lab in South Africa, AI data centres in SA and Nigeria — **and has no consumer AI product and no proprietary African-language data asset.**
- **Ayoba died on 20 March 2026.** MTN currently has *zero* youth engagement product. 35 million MAU, gone.

> **The read:** South Africa's languages are the scarcest, most valuable untapped data asset on the continent, they are owned by the demographic with the least income, and MTN needs them and has no way to get them. That is a three-sided fit that does not come along often.

### How it works

**It is a game, and it must be built as one.**

- **Daily challenges in your language.** Read this sentence. Describe this photo out loud. Say this the way you'd say it to your friend, not the way you'd write it. Argue with this statement for thirty seconds.
- **Code-switch challenges specifically** — the data nobody on earth has.
- **A validation layer.** Other speakers rate and correct submissions. Both sides earn. This is how you get quality without a lab.
- **Streaks, levels, leaderboards** — by language, and by place. *Khayelitsha vs Soweto. Thohoyandou vs Giyani.* Language as sport.
- **Instant payout in MoMo.** Cents per task. This is precisely what mobile money exists for and precisely what card rails cannot do economically.
- **The rarer your language, the more you earn.** Tshivenda (2.5%) and isiNdebele (1.7%) speakers earn the most. Economically correct — scarcity pricing — and morally satisfying: **the smallest, most-ignored language groups become the highest earners.** That inversion is the emotional core of the pitch.

### The technical spine — this is where it stops being a survey

- **Active learning.** The model requests what it is *least certain about*, not random samples. You are not collecting data, you are running a targeted acquisition loop. This is the difference between a corpus and an engine, and it is a real, explainable ML technique.
- **Quality control:** inter-annotator agreement, on-device audio gating (reject before it costs data to upload), statistical outlier detection.
- **Anti-fraud is the hard part, and naming it is credibility.** This *will* be gamed — one person, many accounts, recycled audio. Defences: speaker verification for *uniqueness* (not identity — a legitimate use of a technology that is unsafe for auth), device and SIM checks, payout thresholds, duplicate-audio fingerprinting.
- **Consent as infrastructure.** Contribution consent captured via **Get Consent** — PIN-signed, scoped, revocable. **An ethically-sourced, individually-consented, revocable African language corpus is a thing that does not currently exist anywhere**, and it is a defensible asset precisely because of how it was collected.
- **Output:** a growing licensed corpus, plus fine-tuned models, plus a live WER curve you can put on a slide.

### Only-MTN

- **Cent-scale micro-payments to 69.5m wallets.** No card rail can do this. The entire economics of the product depend on mobile money existing.
- **Identify (KYC) API** solves the hardest problem in crowdsourced data — proving each contributor is a real, unique human — **without you storing a single identity document.**
- **MTN is simultaneously the payer and the buyer.** It funds the data and it needs the data. That is the easiest internal business case in the entire project.
- **Voice-call contribution reaches feature phones.** You can contribute speech over a plain call with no app and no data — which reaches rural, older and *the most linguistically valuable* speakers, who are exactly the ones every other data-collection effort misses.

### The demo

1. A judge speaks a sentence in their own language into a phone.
2. Their name appears on a live national leaderboard. Their township's score ticks up.
3. **Cents land in a MoMo wallet on screen, in real time.**
4. Then the slide that lands it: the model's isiXhosa word error rate, dropping live as contributions come in — starting from Google's 56.71%.

### ⚠️ The honest risk: is this really Track 2?

**It is a work product wearing a game costume, and a judge could say so.**

**Mitigation, and it is not cosmetic — it changes what you build:** lead with the game. Lead with cultural pride. Lead with the leaderboard and the rivalry. **Never open with the words "data annotation."** The opening line is:

> *"South Africa's culture is being harvested for free. This is the app where it pays you back."*

If that framing does not survive contact with the team's own gut, **switch this entry to Track 1** (it defends easily as income/essential) and put a different concept in Track 2. But built as a genuine social game, it is the most novel thing anyone will submit to that track in the competition's history.

### Failure scenarios

| Scenario | Answer |
|---|---|
| **People will game it** | Inter-annotator agreement, uniqueness via speaker verification, device/SIM checks, duplicate-audio fingerprinting, payout thresholds, outlier detection. Assume adversaries from day one. |
| **"This is a digital sweatshop"** | Address it in the pitch, unprompted. Published transparent rates, revocable consent, contributors credited as corpus stakeholders, rates benchmarked openly. Getting asked this and having no answer is fatal; raising it yourself is disarming. |
| **Data quality will be poor** | Active learning + peer validation + on-device audio gating. Quality is a designed property, not a hope. |
| **No smartphone** | Contribute by voice call. Zero data, zero app. |
| **Cold start — no contributors** | Seed with the team's networks and one university partnership. DSFSI at the University of Pretoria built Swivuriso; they are the obvious ally. |
| **Data costs make contributing unaffordable** | On-device compression, gate before upload, and MTN can zero-rate its own data-collection app at effectively no cost. |
| **POPIA / consent** | Get Consent, PIN-signed, per-contribution, revocable. Identify means no stored identity. Better consent hygiene than any Western dataset. |
| **"Why can't Google just do this?"** | Google has no African wallet, cannot pay in cents, cannot verify unique humans at scale, and cannot reach feature phones. It just spent a fortune on WAXAL and *still* skipped South Africa. |

### Scale

Every MTN market has underserved languages — **Nigeria alone has 500+.** Same architecture, same payout rail, new challenge set. And it feeds MTN Group's stated AI strategy directly, in every market it operates.

# PART 4 — TRACK 3 ENTRY

## 🚀 HAMBA — *go*
### From *"hamba kahle"* — go well. The journey someone is watching.

> The name is a farewell every South African says and every South African understands. It already means *travel safely*.

### The one sentence

**Pay for your journey and the network watches you take it — payment held in escrow until you arrive, monitored by cell-tower handovers, working on a feature phone with no app, no GPS and no data.**

### The facts

- **15 million daily minibus taxi commuters, all paying cash. At least 14 prior cashless attempts have failed.**
- **Over 80% of South African ride-hailing trips are paid in cash** (Bolt's own published figure). Drivers carry dangerous amounts of it.
- **Express kidnapping is up 264% since 2014/15, running at roughly 53 a day.** Of 2023/24 kidnappings: **44% linked to hijackings, 22% to robberies, only 4% ransom.** **Nearly 80% of Gauteng kidnappings are tied to armed robbery.** These are acquisitive crimes, and transport is where they happen.
- SABRIC's documented case: an **Uber driver robbed at gunpoint, R6,000 forced transfer.**
- **1.8–2 million gig workers, a $5.03bn economy, ride-hailing 29% of it — and almost none can prove what they earn.**
- **16% of SA connections are feature phones**; MTN SA sold **29% 2G devices** in 2023/24.

> **The read:** the transport payment problem and the transport *safety* problem are the same problem, and everyone has been trying to solve only the first one. The reason the driver wants cash is the reason the driver gets robbed.

### How it works

1. **Start a journey.** USSD, voice, or scan a code at the rank. Origin and destination declared. **No app required.**
2. **Payment goes into escrow.** The driver sees funds are committed — that is the trust unlock. **The cash leaves the vehicle.**
3. **The network watches.** Cell-tower handover sequence, not GPS. No app, no data, no battery drain. Expected route corridor and duration learned from aggregate journeys on that route.
4. **Arrive → escrow releases automatically.** Both sides done, no confirmation ritual.
5. **Something's wrong** — the corridor deviates hard, the journey stops where journeys don't stop, or duration blows past the learned envelope → **a tiered escalation**: silent in-app/USSD check-in first, then nominated contacts, then a response partner.

**The driver gets:** no cash in the car, guaranteed payment, and a **provable income record** — the thing two million gig workers cannot get and the gateway to every financial product MTN wants to sell them.
**The passenger gets:** a journey somebody is watching, and no cash on them either.

### The technical spine

- **Route and duration models learned from aggregate tower-handover sequences.** No GPS, no map data required — the network already produces the signal.
- **Sequence anomaly detection** on journey trajectories. This is a well-posed, well-understood ML problem, not a research gamble.
- **Risk scoring by corridor, time of day and route history** — escalation thresholds that adapt rather than a single global rule.
- **The exhaust: a live feed of the informal transit network.** ⚠️ **Be precise here.** The SA minibus network *has* been mapped — WhereIsMyTransport did large-scale surveys, TeksiMap and open-data projects exist. **What has never existed is a *live* feed.** Every mapping effort so far is a snapshot that decays. Payments produce a continuous one, for free, as a by-product. That is the honest and still-remarkable claim.

### Only-MTN — the purest in the whole project

**Cell-tower handover data.** Uber doesn't have it. Bolt doesn't have it. Shop2Shop, Capitec, Yoco, TymeBank — none of them. **You cannot build this without owning the radio network.** Umoya scores five on Only-MTN; Hamba scores five and there is no argument to be had about it.

### Why this beats the 14 failures

Every one of them **tried to replace the fare** and therefore needed the taxi industry's permission, a terminal at ~R2,000/month, and behaviour change from three parties at once.

**Hamba replaces nothing.** It adds safety and guaranteed payment on top of a journey that happens anyway. It works driver-to-passenger with no association, no terminal and no platform. And the driver benefit — *no cash in the car, in a country where drivers are killed for cash* — is immediate, selfish, and requires no trust in the passenger whatsoever.

That last sentence is the pitch. Say it exactly like that.

### The demo

1. Two phones. **One of them a R149 feature phone.**
2. Start a journey by USSD. No app installed on anything.
3. **Walk out of the room.** On the big screen, the journey tracks by cell.
4. Deviate — walk the wrong way, or stop and stand still. **The escalation fires.** The nominated contact's phone lights up on stage.
5. Return. Escrow releases. Driver paid.

### Failure scenarios

| Scenario | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Tower location is too coarse** | **Correct — and say so first.** Tower granularity is hundreds of metres to kilometres. That is enough for *corridor and duration* anomaly detection; it is **not** turn-by-turn tracking, and claiming otherwise will get you caught. Design thresholds accordingly. Smartphone users can optionally add GPS for precision. |
| **MTN may not expose tower data** | Same play as Umoya: build against a simulated handover feed, show it working, and **pitch the data contract as the ask.** A partnership proposal, not a gap. |
| **False alarms will destroy trust** | Tiered escalation starting with a *silent* check-in, never a siren. Thresholds learned per corridor. A product that cries wolf is worse than no product. |
| **Privacy / POPIA** | **Monitoring only while a journey is active.** Journey-scoped consent via Get Consent, PIN-signed and revocable, data retained only for the journey window. This constraint is not a compromise — it is the design, and it must be explicit on a slide before anyone asks. |
| **Driver won't accept escrow** | They are paid instantly on arrival and carry no cash in a country where drivers are killed for cash. Test the assumption in week one — but the incentive is real and selfish. |
| **Taxi association politics** | Not required for v1. Works e-hailing, private hire and lift clubs, driver-to-passenger. The minibus industry is phase two, entered from a position of proof. |
| **No network in a dead zone** | Journey state is held network-side; gaps in handover data are treated as missing observations, not as alarms. Escalate on *sustained* anomaly, never on a single gap. |
| **What about the 20% who already pay by card?** | Not the market. The 80% paying cash is. |
| **Is this fintech or is it safety?** | The payment *is* the mechanism. Escrow is the safety instrument. Remove the money and there is no product. |

### Scale

Every MTN market has an informal transport economy and a personal-safety problem. The architecture is the same everywhere; only the corridor models retrain.

> **MTN's real asset is not the wallet. It is the network — and nobody has ever built consumer products out of it.**
>

### If a judge asks "why three?"
