# PART A — THE HACKATHON

## A1. This edition

- **MoMo Mini App Hackathon 2026** · 2–3 September 2026 · Johannesburg · in person · 24 hours
- Teams of up to 4, or individuals. 18+. Country-locked (SA entrants compete in SA).
- USD 10,000 prize pool across 1st/2nd/3rd.
- Three tracks: Everyday Essentials · Entertainment & Lifestyle · Travel & Mobility.
- Framing quote from MTN: *"Don't build another app. Build the next service millions of Africans will access through MoMo."*
- Explicit promise beyond prizes: prototype→production support, access to MoMo experts, **marketplace opportunities**, **potential integration into the future MoMo ecosystem.**

**Note the language shift.** Previous editions were "MoMo API Hackathon" — build *anything* on our APIs. This one is the **"Mini App"** Hackathon. The unit of output has changed from *an app that uses MoMo* to *a service that lives inside MoMo*. That is a product decision, and it tells you what to build.

## A2. Lineage — the four editions

| Edition | When | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| 1st MoMo API Hackathon | ~2021–22 | Country-run (e.g. Zambia via BongoHive), bootcamp model |
| 2nd MoMo API Hackathon | ~2022 | Group-wide |
| 3rd Open API Hackathon | 31 Jul – 9 Oct 2023 | 14–15 countries, online (HackerEarth), 5-week ideation → top 12 per country → prototyping phase. SA participated for the first time; ~3,990 SA registrations. |
| **4th — Mini App Hackathon** | **2–3 Sept 2026** | **In person, 24 hrs, track-based, mini-app-native** |

The format change from *10-week online* to *24-hour in-person* matters enormously: **a 24-hour build cannot be deep.** It can only be sharp. Scope brutally.

## A3. Every known past winner

**South Africa 2023** — all three winners were credit/lending plays:
1. **Zaka** — cashflow financing: merchants instantly cash out point-of-sale revenue into a MoMo wallet.
2. **Tata Imali** — crowdfunded micro-lending for low-income earners with no credit history.
3. **MoLo** — P2P lending marketplace; lenders set terms, borrowers accept.

**Nigeria 2023:**
1. **Rova Pay** — automated USSD payments requiring **no internet and no manual code entry**. Used MoMo v2.1 (Notification, KYC, Authentication) + v2.2 (Channel as a Service).
2. **MoMo Cash** — rewards: earn MoMo coins on transactions, redeem against future bills. Gamified loyalty.
3. **Clinique Plus Pay** — ring-fenced health savings inside the MoMo wallet, built for medical expenses.

**Uganda 2023:**
1. **Kabokisi** — digital savings: goals, group fundraising, auctions.
2. **EchoKash** — **AI voice-driven transactions**: buy airtime, check balance, get a mini statement by speaking.
3. **SharePay** — group bill splitting.

### What the winner list actually teaches

1. **Savings and credit dominated — and are now absent from the 2026 tracks.** Do not bring a lending app to a track list with no lending track. The judges have been redirected.
2. **Access-layer innovation wins.** Rova Pay won Nigeria for *removing* the internet requirement. EchoKash placed for *removing* the screen. In markets where the constraint is access, the interface **is** the innovation.
3. **Nobody has ever won on entertainment, mobility, or commerce.** Tracks 2 and 3 are effectively virgin ground in this competition's history. Less competition, more novelty credit, and precisely the categories MTN's super app needs.
4. **Every winner was a single, nameable verb.** Cash out. Pay by voice. Split the bill. Save for medicine. Not a platform. Not a suite. One verb.

# PART C — THE MOMO API SURFACE

*(Full technical detail in `MOMO_API_AND_MINIAPP.md`. Summary here.)*

MTN publishes **nine** API product families — far more than the "collections and disbursements" most developers know:

| # | Product | What it does | Under-used potential |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Get Paid** (Collections) | Request payment, refund, notify, status, balance | The default. Everyone uses this. |
| 2 | **Pay** (Disbursements) | Transfer to wallets, status, balance. Salaries, benefits, supplier payments | Payouts, rewards, refunds, revenue-share |
| 3 | **Invoice** | Bill a customer for **deferred payment** across channels | ⭐ Credit-like UX without being a lender. Almost nobody uses this. |
| 4 | **Manage** | Balance, transaction status, reconciliation | Merchant bookkeeping |
| 5 | **Distribute** | Resell MTN/MoMo services — cash-in/out, airtime — on commission | ⭐ Turn any user into a mini-agent |
| 6 | **Interact** — *Channel as a Service* | **Insert your service into the MoMo USSD and app menus** | ⭐⭐ Distribution to 69.5m users including feature phones. This is the most powerful and least understood API MTN has. |
| 7 | **Identify** (KYC) | Get verified customer identity **without collecting it yourself** | ⭐ Instant onboarding, zero KYC burden, no data liability |
| 8 | **Get Consent** | USSD-based terms acceptance authenticated with the **MoMo PIN** | ⭐⭐ A *cryptographically-adjacent mandate primitive*. This is standing authority. See Part F. |
| 9 | **Notify** | Transactional SMS after payment | Reach users with no data |

Plus: **Remittances** (cross-border) and the **Collection Widget** (QR-code payment on any website).

### The three sleepers

Most hackathon entries will use Get Paid and stop. The differentiated plays live in:

- **Interact / Channel as a Service** — you are not building an app users must find. You are building a menu item inside the thing they already open. Rova Pay won Nigeria partly on this.
- **Get Consent** — a PIN-authenticated, USSD-delivered consent record. Combine it with Invoice and Pay and you have **programmable standing authority over a wallet**. Nobody has built on this.
- **Identify** — zero-friction onboarding. In a market where KYC kills conversion, "sign up with MoMo" is a superpower.

## C1. The Mini App programme itself

- MTN runs a **MoMo Mini App Program** on its developer community, and the integration model is a **PWA** — "MoMo PWA Mini App Integration."
- The published scope covers: end-to-end technical integration requirements, **UX and design standards**, mandatory **security and compliance controls**, and T&Cs for ecosystem participation.
- **The detailed docs are gated / thin publicly.** ⚠️ **Action item: register a MoMo developer account and pull the full PWA Mini App spec before 2 September.** Whoever has actually read the design standards will out-build the room, because judges from MTN will recognise compliance with their own guidelines instantly.

**Implication for the build:** a MoMo mini app is a web app. React/Vue/Svelte + a service worker. No app store, no native build, no install. That is very good news for a 24-hour hackathon — and it means **your differentiation cannot be the tech stack.** It has to be the idea and the execution polish.

- **April 2026:** Safaricom merged M-PESA and MySafaricom into **My OneApp**, with built-in mini apps.

- Public developer platform: `mpesaminiapps.safaricom.co.ke` with full docs.

**MTN is behind.** Safaricom has 221 mini apps live; MTN's mini app programme is a documentation page. MTN knows this. Your pitch can say so, respectfully — "M-PESA has 221 of these; MoMo needs its first ten, and this is one of them" is a devastatingly effective line if delivered without arrogance.

## D2. VodaPay — the cautionary tale in MTN's own backyard

- Vodacom launched VodaPay in SA in 2021, built on **Alipay mini-program technology** — the *same technology family MTN has now licensed from Ant*.
- Heavily marketed as SA's first super app. Broadly considered to have underdelivered against the hype.

**The lesson MTN must be terrified of repeating:** *a super app is not made by having a mini app platform.* VodaPay had the shelf and the tech and the marketing budget. What it lacked was mini apps people needed **weekly**. If your concept cannot answer *"why would someone open this every week?"* it is a VodaPay tile and the judges — who live in Johannesburg and watched this happen — will feel it even if they can't articulate it.

## D3. Shop2Shop — who is actually winning the SA informal economy

- **177,000 traders**, **114,000 acquiring devices**, **2,900 smart safes**, **R131bn/yr** in supplier payments.

- Positions itself as *"a financial management system for small businesses"* — not a payment processor. Bookkeeping, stock ordering, supplier payments, all native.
- CEO Peter Berry's thesis: **"Once that layer [taxi payments] is solved, cash will disappear."**

**This is the most important competitive fact in South Africa.** Shop2Shop is capturing the merchant relationship with *software*, while MoMo captures it with *acceptance*. Software wins the relationship. MoMo has 2.2m merchants and gives them almost no tooling.

# PART E — SOUTH AFRICAN MARKET REALITY

## E1. The informal economy is the market

- Informal market valued at **R900bn – R1.5 trillion**.
- **Spaza shop sector: R190bn.**
- **Township fast food (*kasi kos*): R90bn.**
- Cash still dominates retail payments despite rapid digital growth.

## E2. The taxi problem — the biggest unsolved payment problem in Africa

- **15 million daily minibus taxi commuters. All of them pay cash.**
- **At least 14 prior cashless taxi attempts have failed.**
- Failure causes, per Waxd CEO Anthony Stewart: political tension inside taxi associations; systems that ignored operational reality (*one lacked the ability to pay for fuel*); no education across drivers, owners and passengers. *"Change in any industry causes a lot of anxiety."*
- Current attempts: tap-and-go terminals at **~R2,000/month** per taxi. Gauteng's Premier has called for full transition by 2026.
- Upside offered to drivers: proof of income → bank recognition, formal employment, UIF, workers' compensation, trip insurance, R3–4k/month savings on finance repayments.

**Why this matters even if you don't build a taxi app:** as long as commuters must hold cash for taxis, they withdraw cash, and once they hold cash they spend it at the spaza. **The taxi is the pump that keeps cash circulating in the township economy.** Every digital payment product in SA is fighting the taxi, directly or indirectly.

**And note the trap:** 14 teams smarter and better funded than a hackathon team have failed at this head-on. The winning move is almost certainly *oblique* — see Part F, Concept 2.

## E3. The spaza shift — a live, urgent, 2026 window

- Hundreds of foreign-owned spaza shops have closed across KZN, Gauteng and the Western Cape following anti-immigrant protests; local South Africans are taking over.
- **Prices are spiking** — bread from ~R16 to ~R30 in some communities — as new owners lack the buying networks, stock discipline and margin management of the operators they replaced.
- Government and fintechs are positioning technology adoption as the fix.

**This is a genuinely fresh, dated, local opportunity.** Thousands of first-time shop owners, this year, with no systems, no supplier credit, no pricing intelligence, no stock control — and a MoMo wallet in their pocket. That is a *cohort*, and cohorts are what platforms are built on.

## E4. Structural constraints to design around

- **~600 million sub-Saharan Africans lack reliable electricity.** Battery and data are scarce resources. A PWA that assumes a charged phone on LTE is designing for the wrong continent.
- Data cost remains a real barrier — lightweight assets are not a nicety.
- SIM-swap and social-engineering fraud is endemic (see E5) — trust is a *product feature*, not a compliance checkbox.

## E5. Fraud — the crisis MTN is paying Ant to fix

- **~US$4bn/year** siphoned from Africa in digital financial crime; identity theft is **63%** of it.
- **SIM swapping alone accounts for ~43% of all mobile money fraud.**
- **90% of mobile money providers** have been hit by identity fraud.
- **MTN Nigeria lost US$53m** to mobile money fraud in 2022. Safaricom lost $4m+ to SIM fraud in 2022.
- **South Africa: +86% surge in digital banking crime; ~R1.888bn in losses.**
- Most African countries have **no regulatory requirement to verify identity on SIM replacement.**
- Proven intervention: Tanzania's 2023 agent training programme cut agent-assisted fraud **51%**.

**Ant International's contribution to the MoMo deal explicitly includes fraud prevention.** Fraud is on MTN's executive agenda *right now*. An entry that meaningfully reduces fraud is speaking the judges' language.

## E6. The number that should shape your entire idea

> **Over 70% of registered mobile money accounts in Africa are dormant. Only 25.7% are active in a given month.**
> (GSMA 2026 — mobile money passed **US$2 trillion** in transactions in 2025; sub-Saharan Africa alone ~$1.4tn.)

MTN's problem is **not acquisition**. It has 13m registered users in South Africa and openly says it has stopped chasing more. Its problem is **that most wallets are empty most of the time and most apps are unopened most of the week.**

Additional context on the dormancy problem:
- Usage gender gap persists even where ownership is equal (Uganda: 29% of male account holders took mobile loans vs 16% of women).
- Mobile money **taxes** in Cameroon, Mali and Senegal pushed users back to cash; Ghana cut its e-levy in April 2025 after three years of decline.
- **SSA remittance costs run 8.78% against the UN's 3% target.**

**Frame your pitch around dormancy and you are answering the question MTN is actually asking.**

## F2. Six wild concepts

*Provocations, not final answers. Full track-by-track ideation goes in `../02_ideas/IDEAS_BOARD.md`.*

### ⚡ WILD 1 — "MoMo Mandate": programmable standing authority
**Track:** Everyday Essentials · **Gap:** 11, 1

MTN shipped a **Get Consent** API — a PIN-authenticated consent record delivered over USSD — and nobody built on it. Combine `Get Consent` + `Invoice` + `Pay` and you have something Africa structurally lacks: **direct debit for the unbanked.**

A user sets a rule once: *"Buy R100 of electricity whenever my meter drops below 20 units, max twice a month."* *"Send gogo R200 airtime on the 1st."* *"Pay school fees in 4 automatic instalments."* The mandate is scoped, capped, revocable, and PIN-signed.

Then extend it: 2026's agentic-payment protocols (AP2, x402) are all about giving AI agents **scoped, revocable spending mandates**. MoMo could be the first African wallet with an agent-mandate layer — and it would be built on rails MTN already owns.

**Why MTN wants it:** recurring transactions = predictable float and revenue. Money that moves automatically is money that isn't dormant. And it's a *primitive* — every other mini app can build on it. That's platform thinking, which is exactly what a company launching a mini app platform wants to hear.

---

### ⚡ WILD 2 — "Change": beat the taxi problem by refusing to fight it
**Track:** Travel & Mobility · **Gap:** 7, 1, 4

Fourteen teams — better funded than us — have failed trying to replace the taxi fare. **So don't touch the fare.** Digitise the **change**.

Every cash transaction in the township economy has the same friction: coins. The driver has no change. The spaza rounds up. You lose R2–R5 a day and nobody thinks about it. Instead of coins, the driver or shopkeeper flicks your change into your MoMo wallet in one second — USSD, QR, or tone.

- **Passenger:** pays cash exactly as always. Zero behaviour change. Gets change that doesn't vanish and accumulates into a real balance.
- **Driver / trader:** never needs a coin float again. Keeps every note.
- **MTN:** a **cash-to-digital pump firing millions of times a day**, and every single user receives a small wallet credit *daily* — which is a daily reason to open MoMo. Gap #1 solved as a side effect.

No taxi association negotiation. No R2,000/month terminal. No infrastructure. It is a Trojan horse into the exact economy 14 teams could not enter through the front door.

---

### ⚡ WILD 3 — "MoMo Shield": the moat only a telco can dig
**Track:** Everyday Essentials · **Gap:** 3

MTN is simultaneously the **network operator** and the **wallet**. It knows things no competitor can know: SIM age, **SIM-swap events**, device changes, tower location, network tenure, call-graph density.

A mini app that sits in front of every risky transfer and shows a trust card:
> ⚠️ *This number was SIM-swapped 6 hours ago.*
> ⚠️ *New to the network 2 days ago.*
> ⚠️ *12 users have reported this number.*
> ✅ *You've sent to this number 14 times since 2023.*

Plus a **30-minute reversible send** for first-time recipients — a cooling-off window that kills social-engineering scams outright.

**Why it's strategically explosive:** SIM swap is **43% of all mobile money fraud**, MTN Nigeria lost **$53m** in one year, and fraud prevention is written into the Ant International deal. Capitec, Shop2Shop, OPay and every neobank in Africa **physically cannot build this** — they don't own the SIM. It converts MTN's telco legacy from a cost centre into its single defensible fintech advantage.

Bigger version: offer it as a **platform service** to every other mini app — "Shield-verified" — and you haven't built an app, you've built infrastructure.

---

### ⚡ WILD 4 — "Sound Money": payments that need no internet, screen or camera
**Track:** Everyday Essentials · **Gap:** 4

Data-over-audio. Two phones transact by playing a tone. No data, no NFC, no QR camera, no Bluetooth pairing, no smartphone required on both sides. Confirmation lands via the **Notify** (SMS) API.

Precedent is directly on the record: **Rova Pay won Nigeria 2023** for removing the internet requirement. **EchoKash placed in Uganda** for removing the screen. In markets where the binding constraint is access, **the interface is the innovation** — and this removes internet, screen *and* camera at once.

600 million sub-Saharan Africans lack reliable electricity. Every competitor's mini app assumes a charged smartphone on LTE. This one doesn't.

---

### ⚡ WILD 5 — "Kasi OS": own the merchant with software, not a card reader
**Track:** Everyday Essentials · **Gap:** 5, 12

Shop2Shop proved the thesis: **software owns the merchant relationship; acceptance does not.** R172bn flows through its ecosystem because it does bookkeeping, stock ordering and supplier payments — not because it takes cards.

And there is a live, dated, 2026 cohort: **thousands of first-time local spaza owners** who took over shops this year, with no buying networks, no stock discipline and no margin control — which is precisely why bread went from R16 to R30 in some communities.

The mini app: photograph your shelf → stock is read and tracked → reorder suggested → **group-buy with nearby shops through MoMo escrow** to reach wholesale pricing → margin tracked per line. Group buying is the **largest mini-program category in China** and has never been built for township retail.

MTN gets 2.2 million merchants opening MoMo daily for something other than accepting a payment.

---

### ⚡ WILD 6 — "MoMo Passport": the roaming wallet
**Track:** Travel & Mobility · **Gap:** 9

MTN operates in **16 markets** and treats cross-border as *remittance* — a one-way pipe for diaspora money. It has never built the layer for the people who actually move: cross-border traders (SA↔Zimbabwe↔Mozambique↔Zambia), students, seasonal workers, bus travellers.

Cross a border and your wallet re-denominates and becomes locally spendable. Local data bundle pre-loaded. Cross-border bus and taxi tickets bookable. Travel micro-insurance attached. Settlement near interbank instead of **8.78%**.

**No competitor can copy this** — it requires MTN's actual physical footprint. It is the single most unarguable strategic-fit idea available in Track 3.

---

### 🚀 THE SKYROCKET — "Ubuntu": social underwriting as a financial primitive
**Track:** Everyday Essentials (framed as community access) · **Gap:** 6, 1, 12

Every past SA winner tried to solve credit for the credit-invisible. **All of them tried to be the lender.** That's the mistake.

Make the community the credit bureau, with **skin in the game**. I stake R50 of my own balance to vouch for your R500. You repay → I earn yield. You default → I lose my stake. Vouching is not a favour; it is an investment, and it is priced.

Underneath, MTN's own network graph does the underwriting — who sends money to whom, how often, for how long, how stable the relationship is. **MTN already holds this data and monetises none of it.**

What it produces:
- **Credit for the credit-invisible** — the problem Tata Imali, MoLo and Zaka all attacked and none solved structurally
- **A reputation worth protecting on MoMo** — the strongest lock-in mechanic in consumer fintech
- **Yield for idle balances** — attacking dormancy from the *supply* side, not just the demand side
- **A new asset class** MTN could aggregate, price and securitise — meaningful for a fintech arm being prepped for separation and listing

This is stokvel logic — which South Africa already runs at R50bn+ scale — rebuilt as a protocol, with peer staking and network-graph underwriting. It is not a feature. **It is how money worked in African communities before banks, rebuilt as infrastructure.**

⚠️ **Caveat to test:** the 2026 tracks contain no lending category, which may be deliberate. Frame it as *community savings and access*, never as "a lending app," or it reads as the thing they steered away from.
