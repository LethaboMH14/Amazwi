## 1. Phone theft, robbery and the financial consequences

### Volume of phone theft

There is **no SAPS crime category for "cellphone theft"** — SAPS records robbery/theft generically, so no official annual phone-theft count exists. The most-cited hard number comes from ISS analysis of reported-stolen/blacklisting data:

| Figure | Value | Period | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mobiles reported stolen | **412,998** | Apr 2017 – Mar 2023 (6 yrs) | [ISS Africa](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/cellphone-theft-another-indicator-of-south-africas-policing-gaps), pub. 21 Nov 2023 |
| Daily average | **189 phones/day** | same period | same |
| Share blacklisted with operators | **only 29%** | same period | same |
| Provincial split | Gauteng 29%, W Cape 26%, KZN 20%, E Cape 7% | same period | same |

⚠️ **Caveat:** 412,998 ÷ 6 years ≈ 68,800/year *reported*. True volume is far higher — the same ISS work and the Victims of Crime Survey both show most thefts are never reported. Treat 189/day as a **reported-crime floor, not the real rate.**

**Victims of Crime / GPSJS 2024/25** ([StatsSA media release](https://www.statssa.gov.za/publications/P0341/Media%20release%20_GPSJS%202024-25%20FNL%2025082025.pdf), data 2024/25, released 26 Aug 2025):
- **1.3 million incidents** of theft of personal property in 2024/25
- **1.2 million victims** aged 16+ (**2.6%** of that population)
- **Only ~31%** of victims reported some or all incidents to police
- Home robbery: **259,000 incidents**, 213,000 households (1.1%); 51.3% reported

⚠️ The 2024/25 GPSJS release does **not** break out cellphones as a separate item. The "personal property theft" line is the closest official proxy.

### "Kidnap and force to transfer" (express kidnapping)

This is a **verified, named, rising trend** — strongest angle in this section.

- **264% increase** in kidnappings 2014/15 → 2023/24, reaching **17,061 incidents** (ISS, via [Daily Maverick, 19 Apr 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-04-19-express-kidnappings-skyrocket-steven-gruzd-murder-raises-alarm-over-increase-in-abductions/))
- Of 2023/24 kidnappings: **44% linked to hijackings, 22% to robberies, only 4% involved ransom demands** — i.e. the overwhelming majority are acquisitive, not classic ransom (same source)
- Kidnapping has **almost doubled since 2021/22**: Q3 cases went 2,605 (2021/22) → **4,775** (2025/26); Gauteng = over half nationally ([Daily Maverick, 19 Apr 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-04-19-express-kidnappings-skyrocket-steven-gruzd-murder-raises-alarm-over-increase-in-abductions/); [Parliament, 11 Mar 2026](https://archive.parliament.gov.za/server/api/core/bitstreams/985e64a2-2624-4b13-937e-a276e87daa0c/content))

- Nearly **80% of Gauteng kidnappings are linked to armed robberies** ([TimesLIVE, 23 Mar 2026](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2026-03-23-hijackers-force-banking-app-transfers-in-rising-crime-trend/))
- SABRIC CEO Andre Wentzel: *"These financially motivated abductions typically unfold quickly, with perpetrators detaining individuals just long enough to coerce them into draining their accounts."* Documented case: Uber driver robbed at gunpoint, **R6,000** forced transfer (same TimesLIVE article, 23 Mar 2026)
- ISS's Lizette Lancaster: amounts range *"from a couple of thousand rand to millions"* depending on group sophistication ([TimesLIVE, 23 Mar 2026](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2026-03-23-hijackers-force-banking-app-transfers-in-rising-crime-trend/))

## 2. Load shedding, electricity and prepaid meters in 2026

### Status: load shedding has effectively stopped — but load *reduction* has not

**Load shedding ended on 16 May 2025.** As of 4 August 2026 South Africa had gone **441 consecutive days without load shedding** ([TechFinancials, 4 Aug 2026](https://techfinancials.co.za/2026/08/04/eskom-reports-441-days-without-load-shedding/); confirmed by [SAnews](https://www.sanews.gov.za/south-africa/eskom-reports-441-days-without-load-shedding)).

| Metric | Value | Date |
|---|---|---|
| Days without load shedding | **441** | as at 4 Aug 2026 |
| Peak daily EAF | **82.04%** — highest daily since 2017 | 26 Jul 2026 |
| Year-to-date EAF | **66.97%** (+7.28% y/y) | Jul 2026 |
| Unplanned outages | **4,562 MW** — lowest since 30 Jun 2018 | 26 Jul 2026 |
| Weekly avg unplanned outages | 5,553 MW, down **47.8%** from 10,641 MW | 24–30 Jul 2026 |
| Diesel used | **Zero**, 2nd consecutive week | 24–30 Jul 2026 |
| Eskom forecast | **No load shedding through 31 Aug 2026** | Aug 2026 |

Earlier baseline: FY2025/26 saw **only 26 hours of load shedding in the whole financial year**, with EAF at 67.55% MTD December 2025 vs 57.06% Dec 2024 ([Eskom power system status, 26 Dec 2025](https://www.eskom.co.za/power-system-status/)).

### ⚠️ Critical distinction for your strategy doc: LOAD REDUCTION ≠ LOAD SHEDDING

Load reduction is still running and it hits exactly the low-income townships a fintech/utility product would target:

- **What it is:** Eskom cuts power to *specific overloaded transformers and feeders* physically at risk of burning out — local, not national, and **not announced as stages**
- **When:** two daily windows — **05:00–09:00 and 17:00–22:00** (i.e. morning prep and evening cooking peaks)
- **Scale:** Eskom had **removed 651,828 customers** from load reduction schedules as at late June 2026 — the remaining affected total is not published
- **Target:** Eskom aims to eliminate load reduction in a 7th province by Oct 2026 and achieve **nationwide eradication by 2027**
- *"Two streets in the same town can have completely different winters"* — schedules vary by individual transformer
- Source: [EnergyBee, 29 Jun 2026](https://energybee.co.za/news/load-reduction-schedule-south-africa-2026); eradication target from [TechFinancials, 4 Aug 2026](https://techfinancials.co.za/2026/08/04/eskom-reports-441-days-without-load-shedding/)

### EskomSePush API — yes, programmatically available

### The "electricity advance" problem — verified as a real product category

Prepaid electricity on credit: a provider fronts emergency units immediately and recovers the amount plus a fee from your next recharge.

- **Providers:** BluAdvance/Umhlakasi (`*130*6266#` / `*120*1608#`), Telkom (`*180#`), MTN MoMo (`*120*151#`), Citiq Advance, plus buy-now-settle-later options in **Capitec** and **TymeBank** apps
- **Typical advance:** **R20–R50**, requires an established recharge history
- **Fee:** from **~R5 per advance** (BluAdvance)
- **The trap:** weekly advances accumulate **roughly R250–R350 per year in fees alone**, and each tariff hike means the same money buys fewer units — creating recurring dependency
- Source: [EnergyBee, 22 Jul 2026](https://energybee.co.za/news/electricity-advance-south-africa-2026)

⚠️ This is a **commercial energy blog**, not a regulator or peer-reviewed source. The mechanism and USSD codes are useful and specific, but the R250–R350/yr fee figure is the publisher's own arithmetic. Verify fees directly with providers before quoting.

### Load shedding impact on small business (historical baseline)

From the **Nedbank / township SMME study, survey conducted Feb 2023**, n>200 township small businesses ([PDF](https://personal.nedbank.co.za/content/dam/nedbank/pdfs/Nedbank-Impact-of-Loadshedding-on-SMMEs.pdf)):
- **64%** stopped operations entirely during load shedding
- **66%** shed jobs because of load shedding; **5%** closed down entirely
- Average monthly revenue loss: **just over R11,000 per business**
- Additional monthly costs by sector: engineering/construction R16,607, manufacturing R10,812, agriculture R10,047, food & beverage R6,356
- Only **1 business in the sample** had solar installed at time of survey
- Macro claims in that report: load shedding cost SA *"over R4 billion a day"*; Stage 6 = *"R900m per day"*

⚠️ **This data is from February 2023 and is now stale** given load shedding ended May 2025. Use it as historical framing ("what the last crisis cost"), not as a current condition. I found no equivalent 2025–26 SMME load-shedding impact survey.

### 2G/3G sunset — ⚠️ IMPORTANT CORRECTION TO YOUR BRIEF

**Your brief states "MTN and Vodacom have announced 2G/3G sunset dates." They have not.** The only firm date is a government one, and both operators have publicly declined to commit to their own.

**Government / regulator timeline** ([Connecting Africa, 4 Jun 2024](https://www.connectingafrica.com/regulation/south-africa-pushes-2g-3g-sunset-to-2027), DCDT gazette):
| Milestone | Date |
|---|---|
| Type-approval prohibition for new 2G/3G devices | 30 Sep 2024 |
| Activation prohibition | 31 Dec 2024 |
| Shutdown commencement | 1 Jun 2025 |
| **Total shutdown** | **31 December 2027** |

ICASA reconfirmed the *"when 2027 comes to a close"* target as recently as [7 Aug 2026](https://htxt.co.za/2026/08/is-south-africa-ready-to-sunset-2g-and-3g-at-the-end-of-next-year/).

**MTN South Africa** ([MyBroadband, 13 Jun 2026](https://mybroadband.co.za/news/cellular/653722-mtn-switching-off-3g-in-parts-of-south-africa.html)):
- **No fixed national shutdown date.** Phased, **area-by-area** transition
- *"Targeted network optimisation activities like spectrum refarming may result in the reduction or removal of 3G capacity in specific areas"*
- Explicitly plans to **retain a limited 2G layer** to support customers on legacy devices
- ⚠️ Note: [DataCenterDynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/mtn-to-retire-3g-in-south-africa-by-end-of-2026/) carries a headline "MTN to retire 3G in South Africa by end of 2026" — **this is contradicted by MTN's own June 2026 statement above.** Do not cite the end-2026 date.

**Vodacom** ([MyBroadband, 13 Jun 2026](https://mybroadband.co.za/news/cellular/653722-mtn-switching-off-3g-in-parts-of-south-africa.html); [TechCentral, 4 May 2026](https://techcentral.co.za/why-2g-will-outlast-3g-in-south-africa/280940/); [Hypertext, 7 Aug 2026](https://htxt.co.za/2026/08/is-south-africa-ready-to-sunset-2g-and-3g-at-the-end-of-next-year/)):
- **Has not commenced 3G sunsetting** and has published **no timeline**
- Will decommission **3G before 2G** — *"The current subscriber trajectory suggests that demand for 3G device network support is lower than the demand for 2G devices"*
- Cites network utilisation and customer terminal penetration as gating factors

**Strategic read for your doc: 2G will outlast 3G in South Africa, and 2G is not going away by 2027 in practice.** That is the defensible position, and it's directly supported by Vodacom's stated ordering and MTN's commitment to retain a 2G layer.

Network coverage (to 30 Sep 2025, [ICASA Mar 2026](https://www.icasa.org.za/uploads/files/The-State-of-the-ICT-Sector-Report-of-South-Africa-31-March-2026.pdf)): **3G 99.85%**, **4G/LTE 99.5%** (from 99.1%), **5G 58.0%** population coverage (up from 46.6%).

### Banked / unbanked

Source: [IOL, 4 Feb 2025](https://iol.co.za/news/politics/2025-02-04-financial-inclusion-in-south-africa-37-3-million-adults-banked/), citing the **Financial Sector Conduct Authority (FSCA)**:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|

| **Unbanked adults** | **7,298,789** |
| Previously banked (lapsed) | 1,855,980 |
| **Fully financially excluded** | **2%** of the population |

⚠️ **Reconcile these carefully — the two commonly-quoted numbers measure different things.** 37.39m banked ÷ (37.39m + 7.30m) = **~84% of adults hold a bank account**. The **2%** figure refers to adults with *no formal or informal financial product at all*, not to the unbanked. Quoting "98% banked" would be wrong.

FinScope 2023 corroborates ([Center for Financial Inclusion, 14 May 2025](https://www.centerforfinancialinclusion.org/article/south-africas-inclusion-story-two-decades-of-financial-progress-amid-economic-challenges/)): **98% served by the formal financial sector, but only 84% held bank accounts**, versus 52% with formal access in 2003.

### Underbanked — the "mailbox banking" finding

This is the most useful financial-inclusion stat in the set. Same CFI/FinScope source (pub. 14 May 2025, FinScope 2023 data):
- **~14 million adults practise "mailbox banking"** — accounts used only to receive and immediately withdraw, not to transact
- **76% of grant recipients withdraw their full benefit amount upon receipt**
- **71% of adults primarily use cash** for food and grocery purchases
- **68% of adults received assistance from the state or social networks** by 2023 (vs 37% in 2003)

**Framing: South Africa's problem is not access, it's usage.** 84% have accounts; ~14 million use them as cash pass-throughs.
