# AMAZWI — BUSINESS CASE
### Sponsored language missions, MoMo value movement and honest pilot economics

**Parent:** `00_MASTER_PLAN.md`
**Money/platform owner:** Sbu
**Product counterpart:** Lethabo

---

## 1. THE COMMERCIAL CLAIM

AMAZWI does not claim to sell ASR-ready hours from the competition game. The output lacks verbatim transcripts.

The first offer is:

> **A sponsored language mission that produces consented, quality-filtered voice clips with peer-verified semantic or intent labels, transparent contributor rewards and auditable consent lineage.**

The first high-value design partner could be MTN itself through a **MoMo Moments** deck covering intents such as buy airtime, send money and check a balance.

---

## 2. WHO PAYS

Ranked by near-term credibility:

1. **MTN or another institution funds a targeted language/intent mission.**
2. **Brands or public-interest sponsors fund a named community/language deck.**
3. **Research and speech-technology partners license or curate governed outputs after transcription and quality review.**

Learner subscriptions and proficiency credentials are not priced, built or pitched as current revenue.

---

## 3. WHAT MOMO DOES

### Inbound

A sponsor funds a mission through MoMo Collections when the event sandbox permits it. The campaign records the provider reference, funded amount and provider mode.

### Internal value accounting

An accepted contribution commits part of the campaign budget and credits the speaker's AMAZWI wallet once. Every change is represented by immutable integer-cent events.

### Outbound

The speaker cashes out to MoMo at a transparent threshold that reflects actual minimum amounts and fees. The app never promises a R2 provider transfer after every clip.

The pitch line is:

> **“The reward is credited immediately. MoMo cash-out is transparent and batched at a viable threshold.”**

Say “money crossed MoMo twice” only if both a Collections and Disbursement sandbox leg actually run during the demonstration.

---

## 4. PILOT ECONOMICS

The competition uses configurable illustrative values, not a forecast:

```text
speaker honorarium per eligible contribution   R2.00  illustrative
paid speaker contributions per day                 3  cap
listener/verifier cash                            R0  competition build
provider fee                                  unknown  must be confirmed
cash-out threshold                           unknown  must be confirmed
acceptance rate                              unknown  measure in pilot
expiry/unvalidated rate                      unknown  measure in pilot
transcription/curation cost                  excluded  downstream step
```

The earlier R1,175-per-“validated hour” model is retained only as historical scenario work. It must not be used as a sales price or margin claim because:

- the game output is not transcribed;
- the acceptance rate was assumed;
- the listener policy changed;
- the provider fee is unconfirmed;
- expiry policy has not been costed;
- demand for the resulting output has not been validated.

### Pilot dashboard

Measure:

- funded amount;
- committed rewards;
- eligible contributions and seconds;
- cost per corpus-eligible contribution;
- acceptance, void and expiry rates;
- verifier availability by language;
- cash-out success and fee;
- repeat play with and without a speaker reward.

Those measurements are more credible than a large theoretical market table.

---

## 5. REWARD POSITION

The R2 rate is an **honorarium for an accepted contribution**, not a wage and not a youth-employment solution.

Rules:

- show the amount before recording;
- never reduce it retroactively;
- cap paid contributions;
- do not attach cash to ranking, chance, popularity or a streak;
- do not compare the product to full employment or imply dependable monthly income;
- test whether the small payment crowds out intrinsic play before scaling it.

The listener side intentionally receives points only in the competition build. This simplifies the ledger and avoids paying for low-confidence MCQ labour.

---

## 6. WHY MTN BENEFITS

Use positive alignment:

1. **Daily digital-service engagement:** a short repeatable entertainment interaction inside the Mini App ecosystem.
2. **Visible MoMo value movement:** mission funding, wallet credit and honest settlement state.
3. **Governed voice-intent learning:** a potential first-party design-partner use case for multilingual voice journeys.
4. **Trust:** the receipt makes reward basis, consent and payment traceability visible.
5. **Expansion configuration:** language packs, consent text, campaign rules and payment adapters can change by market without rebuilding the loop.

Do not lead with criticism of Ayoba, historic MoMo activation ratios or an “empty shelf.” Do not assert MTN is already a buyer or owns no relevant data.

---

## 7. LAUNCH MODEL

The first real deployment is a **closed funded cohort**, not a nationwide marketplace.

Why:

- two proficient listeners are required per eligible clip;
- language liquidity is the main feasibility constraint;
- a closed cohort makes reward cost and verification quality measurable;
- campaigns can recruit supply and demand in the same place;
- expansion follows evidence rather than language-count theatre.

Launch with isiZulu and Setswana. Add a language only when native content ownership, consent copy and verifier liquidity are in place.

---

## 8. RISKS AND OPEN NUMBERS

Do not quote as fact until confirmed:

- bulk B2C disbursement fee;
- minimum cash-out amount;
- event-sandbox currency and South African availability;
- acceptance rate;
- verifier response time;
- cost of transcription and downstream curation;
- willingness to pay for a sponsored mission;
- repeat-play lift from cash, points, streak or league;
- buyer demand for the output.

The competition demonstrates a technically sound transaction and evidence loop. It does not prove product-market fit.

---

## 9. THE ASK

Ask MTN for three small next steps:

1. a conversation with the Mini App and MoMo product teams;
2. the actual Collections/Disbursement commercial constraints for South Africa;
3. one contained MoMo voice-intent mission to measure quality, cost and repeat play.

Zero-rating and wider rollout can follow evidence; they are not prerequisites for the hackathon claim.
