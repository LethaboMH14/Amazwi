## 4. PRIMARY LIVE DEMO

Use one speaker device and two known proficient-verifier devices.

1. The speaker accepts the purpose-specific consent.
2. They receive an isiZulu or Setswana card and record a clue.
3. The recording uploads and appears independently on two verifier phones.
4. Each verifier types the intended concept before reveal.
5. Each sees the blocked words and answers the referee question.
6. The contribution becomes `CORPUS_ELIGIBLE`.
7. One reward credit appears.
8. The Voice Value Receipt opens.

Lethabo narrates the human interaction. Sbu monitors provider state and handles recovery.

If room play is healthy, invite the audience into MCQ learner mode after the golden path. Say explicitly:

> “This audience round is the learner game. It moves XP and popularity, not the governed corpus decision. Two proficient free-text verifiers make that decision.”

## 11. Q&A

### “Is this paid data labelling?”

> “It is a game with a governed contribution output. Learners play MCQ for XP; proficient listeners provide the high-confidence semantic check. We do not pretend the verification labour disappears, and only speakers receive the competition honorarium.”

### “What exactly did you collect?”

> “Elicited spontaneous speech with a peer-verified semantic or intent label, audio-quality evidence and consent lineage. Not a verbatim transcript and not yet an ASR-ready hour.”

### “How do you know the language is correct?”

> “We do not claim automated language proof in this prototype. We store the declared language and require proficient same-language listeners for the semantic decision. A future governed export needs an additional language-quality stage.”

### “Why does MCQ not validate?”

> “Two random four-option answers agree by chance 6.25% of the time. MCQ is the learner game. Only two proficient free-text matches count toward `UNDERSTOOD`.”

### “How do you know the speaker did not say the target?”

> “After submitting their guess, both proficient listeners see the target and blocked words and referee the clip. Two violation votes void it. A disagreement goes to review; we do not pretend peer reporting is machine verification.”

### “What happens if there is only one listener?”

> “It remains open and eventually expires as unvalidated. It cannot enter the governed set or trigger the accepted-contribution reward. That is why the pilot is a funded closed cohort, not a nationwide launch.”

### “Is the output useful without transcripts?”

> “Yes for intent-labelled speech, semantic retrieval and later curation. A MoMo Moments deck gives MTN domain labels directly. ASR requires a separate transcription step, which we state rather than hide.”

### “Can consent be withdrawn after payment?”

> “Yes. Earned value remains. Audio is retired from future playback and export; an audit tombstone and financial record remain. We do not claim instant unlearning from models.”

### “Is the Archive public?”

> “No. Raw voice is private by default. The public Impact Map is aggregate. Public audio or attribution would require a separate opt-in consent and moderation system we did not build.”

### “Did real money move?”

Answer from the actual provider mode on screen. Never generalise beyond it.

### “How does this scale?”

> “By funded language cohorts, not by switching on twelve languages. A language launches when native content, consent copy and proficient-verifier liquidity exist. The software loop is configuration; community readiness is not.”

### “What did you build here?”

Both teammates use the same truthful answer based on organiser approval and commit history.
