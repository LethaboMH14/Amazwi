# AMAZWI — BUSINESS CASE
### Answering the criticism: how MTN makes money, why not Duolingo, why inside MoMo, and what makes people stay

**Written:** 2 Sep 2026, in response to judge/mentor criticism on business value.
**Owner:** shared — Sbu owns the money/commercial claims per `05_BUILD.md` §2; Lethabo owns the narrative framing.
**Evidence rule:** every external figure below is sourced and dated. Every economic *structure* is described as a mechanism. **No revenue projection, margin, or unit-economics number is claimed** — that stays inside `03_BUSINESS.md`'s existing rule that R2 is illustrative until provider fees, acceptance and repeat play are measured.

---

## 0. The one-paragraph answer

> AMAZWI is not a language game that happens to pay out. It is a **customer-lifecycle engine for MoMo that pays for itself in MTN's own currency.** Speakers earn credit that can only be spent inside MTN's ecosystem, which makes the reward cost a fraction of its face value; dormant wallets transact again; and the by-product is the intent-labelled South African speech MoMo needs to become voice-operable. Learners — not speakers — are the paying side. MTN's own abandoned super-app experiment is the proof this belongs inside MoMo rather than beside it.

## 2. "How does MTN make money from this?"

Sbu's test is the right one: **MTN must make money, save money, or reduce the risk of losing money.** AMAZWI does all three, through four distinct mechanisms.

### 2.1 The reward is not an expense — it is a voucher against MTN's own inventory

This is the mechanism most people miss, and it is the core of the model.

When AMAZWI credits a speaker, that credit lands in a **MoMo wallet**. Below the cash-out threshold it *cannot leave the ecosystem*. Its natural redemption is airtime and data — **MTN's own product, on MTN's own network, at MTN's own marginal cost.**

So a reward with a face value of R-something costs MTN materially less than that in real terms, because it is redeemed against inventory MTN produces rather than cash MTN must source. This is airline-miles economics: *issue a currency that is only spendable on your own high-margin inventory, in exchange for something you want.* Here, what MTN wants is engagement and language data.

> **Claim discipline:** the *structure* above is sound and is the argument. The actual saving depends on redemption mix, provider fees and airtime wholesale cost — none of which we have measured. Do not put a percentage on stage.

### 2.2 Dormancy reactivation — the "stop losing money" leg

MTN's own terms define the decay: **inactive at 6 months, dormant at 12, and reactivation then requires a physical service-centre visit.** Every wallet on that path is sunk acquisition cost heading to zero, and the rescue path costs staffed hours.

AMAZWI gives a lapsing user a reason to open the wallet that is not "send money to someone" — it is **"earn something today."** A reactivation driven by a game costs cents. A reactivation driven by a service-centre visit costs a person's time.

This maps onto a metric MTN already reports publicly (monthly active users), which makes it measurable rather than rhetorical.

### 2.3 Closing the monetisation gap: pushing balances toward high-margin rails

Transaction **value** grew 33.8% while **revenue** grew 1.4%. That gap says the volume is concentrated in thin-margin movement. The fat margins in this business are **merchant payments** and **airtime/data**.

AMAZWI's rewards arrive as small balances with a natural spend path into exactly those two rails, across a network of **2.3m merchants** that already exists. It does not just create activity — it creates activity of the *shape* MTN needs.

### 2.4 The strategic asset: making MoMo voice-operable

Voice revenue fell **41%**. Meanwhile MoMo's interface is menu-and-English-shaped, in a country with eleven official languages.

AMAZWI's output is **peer-verified intent labels on real South African speech** — not transcripts, and we must keep saying so. But intent labels are precisely what a voice-driven wallet needs: *buy airtime · send money · check balance*. A MoMo Moments deck of exactly those utterances, in isiZulu and Setswana, is a directly usable asset.

That reframes the whole thing: **MTN would be training its next interface using its own customers, who are paid in currency that returns to MTN.**

## 3. "Shouldn't this be its own app?" — MTN already answered this

**In March 2026 MTN began shutting down Ayoba.** It peaked around **35 million users**, it was **zero-rated**, and it still could not "overcome the deeply entrenched network effects of global tech incumbents." MTN's replacement bet is explicitly a **super app built on MoMo**, with Ant International, starting in Nigeria.

So:

- Building AMAZWI standalone means repeating the exact experiment MTN abandoned five months ago, without MTN's budget or its zero-rating.
- MTN does not need another destination app. It needs **reasons to open MoMo** — that is what the super-app pivot is *for*, and a mini-app platform with no compelling mini apps is an empty shelf.

**And structurally:** "earn" is meaningless without a wallet. Inside MoMo, a reward is instantly spendable. Standalone, it is a number you must withdraw — which means integrating a payment rail anyway, i.e. rebuilding MoMo badly.

> **The wallet is not our distribution channel. It is our game mechanic.**

### "People won't go to MoMo to play a game"

Correct — and they don't have to. **They already go to MoMo for money: 13 billion times in six months.** AMAZWI doesn't ask anyone to open a new app for a game. It puts a reason to *earn* inside the app they already open. The game is why the money exists; it is not the reason for the visit.

## 4. "Why not Duolingo?" — the honest difference

| | Duolingo | AMAZWI |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | You consume a course | Speakers **produce**, learners **consume** |
| Who pays whom | You pay them | Learners pay; **speakers get paid** |
| Content origin | Staff linguists, static | **The community's own voices, growing daily** |
| SA languages | Thin to absent at depth | isiZulu + Setswana first-class, natives-only verified |
| Payment rail | None needed | **The rail is the product** |
| What it leaves behind | Your streak | **A governed national language corpus** |

**The moat is not the game — it is the rail.** Duolingo can add an isiZulu course next quarter. What it cannot do is pay a Setswana speaker in Mahikeng R-something into a wallet they already have and already trust, and have it spendable on airtime that evening. That requires a mobile-money licence, an agent network and a national float. **MTN has those. A language app cannot acquire them.**

Second moat: **living language.** A static course teaches textbook Setswana. AMAZWI captures how people actually speak this year — the loanwords, the code-switching, the township register. That corpus appreciates; a course depreciates.

## 5. Who actually pays — the two-sided market

The mistake is assuming players pay. They don't. There are four revenue sides and the players are the *supply*.

### Side A — Speakers (supply, earn)
Proficient speakers. They produce the corpus. **Earning is never paywalled** — paywalling it would be exploitative and would strangle supply. Cost to MTN is low for the reasons in §2.1.

### Side B — Learners (demand, PAY) ← the subscription engine
This is where recurring revenue lives, and the strongest segment is not who people expect:

- **Urban South African parents whose children cannot speak their home language.** This is a widespread, emotionally loaded anxiety in SA households. It is the "kids play, parents pay" model — and unlike a generic edtech pitch, the content is *the child's own grandmother's dialect*, not a synthetic voice.
- **Diaspora families** keeping children connected to a home language.
- **Working professionals** who need workplace language: nurses, police, teachers, call-centre staff.
- **Corporates** — language and transformation training carries real budget in SA.

The decisive structural advantage: **Side B's content is manufactured by Side A.** Duolingo pays linguists to build courses. AMAZWI's course material is produced by users paid in scrip that largely returns to MTN. That is a fundamentally cheaper content engine, and it improves as it scales.

### Side C — Sponsored missions (brand marketing budget)
A brand funds a language mission through MoMo Collections. This is media spend with measurable engagement, not CSR. It also puts money *into* the rail — the funding leg is a real Collections transaction.

### Side D — Governed data licensing (enterprise)
Banks, insurers, government and researchers needing consented, provenance-tracked SA-language speech. This is the slowest and highest-value leg, and the one that most needs the governance work already built (consent lineage, export firewall, revocation). **Not a day-one claim.**

## 6. What makes people stay — the retention ladder

Modelled on the pull-not-push pattern (Claude's free tier; never coercive):

**The hook (day 1).** You speak your own language for thirty seconds and money appears in a wallet you already have. For a low-income user in South Africa **airtime is functionally currency** — earning it by speaking your own language is both novel and dignifying. That is the emotional core of the whole product.

**The habit (week 1).**
- **Daily streaks** on a small free allowance (e.g. a few cards a day, all earning).
- **The Duel** — challenge a specific friend to your card. This is the mechanic in the Kuest reference (`04_assets/reference/`, "Duel Invitation → Accept/Decline"), and it is *social acquisition*: users bring their own network, paid for in MTN scrip. **Reverse CAC.**
- **Regional leaderboards** — province against province. Civic pride is free marketing, and it gives premiers and municipalities a reason to care.

**The meaning (month 1).**
- *"Your voice taught 400 people this week."* The Impact Map turns contribution into visible national contribution.
- Language preservation is not a feature here; it is the reason people forgive the app for being a game.

**The conversion (the paying moment).** Not on the speaker side. It happens when a **parent** sees *"your child completed their first full Setswana conversation"* — that notification is the subscription trigger, and it converts on emotion, not utility.

## 7. Scalability — answering the criticism directly

**The software is configuration; the community is not.** A new language launches when three things exist: native-reviewed cards, consent copy in that language, and enough proficient verifiers for liquidity. That is a real constraint and we should say so rather than claim instant national scale.

But the *rails* scale immediately:
- **70.8m MoMo MAU** across MTN's footprint — no new distribution to build.
- **1.4m agents** — a physical onboarding network that already exists, and an angle worth exploring: agents running "language corners" for commission.
- The same mechanic works in **any MTN market with a language gap**, which is most of them. Nigeria (the super-app launch market) has Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo — the identical problem shape.

**Honest limit to state on stage:** we prove a closed-cohort loop, not nationwide liquidity. Two quality-assured languages, not twelve. Saying that *increases* credibility with technical judges rather than reducing it.

## 9. Open items — deliberately not claimed

- **No revenue, margin, or savings percentage** is modelled. The structures in §2 are mechanisms, not forecasts. Sbu owns any number that reaches a slide.
- **Voice-as-proof-of-life for social grants** is an obvious adjacency (voice + verified wallet identity) and is deliberately excluded — it touches vulnerable beneficiaries and would need its own consent, legal and ethics work.
- **Voice biometrics / authentication** is likewise excluded: it collides with the project's standing prohibition on synthesis and voice-cloning lineage, and would need separate consent.
- **Agent "language corners"** (§7) is an untested distribution idea, not a plan.
- Side D (data licensing) is the highest-value and slowest leg; it must not be presented as near-term revenue.
